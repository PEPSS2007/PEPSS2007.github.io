Off-grid emergency alert device

Case study

Problem

Emergency alerts usually depend on internet or cellular networks, which may not exist where help is needed.

Solution

GrievanceTriage

Municipal grievance triage and resolution platform

Next.jsTypeScriptSupabaseLeafletGemini API

Takes citizen complaints from several channels, triages them with AI and rules, plots them on a map, and tracks each one to verified resolution.

Case study

Problem

Municipal bodies receive complaints through many channels and must route them to the right department, spot emergencies, and confirm that the work was actually done.

Solution

A triage pipeline redacts personal data, flags prompt-injection attempts, then applies rules and an LLM with a fallback, labelling every result as ai, fallback or rules. Uploaded photos get a vision analysis. Complaints appear on a Leaflet map, resolve to ward-level officer contacts, and move through a multi-stage resolution workflow with an audit trail.

Highlights

  • 4 intake channels: citizen portal, officer entry, bulk CSV, secure webhook
  • 7-stage lifecycle from Submitted to Resolved or Reopened
  • Audit event recorded for every administrative action
  • 57 test assertions across 11 suites, per the project README
  • Runs in a demo mode with seeded sample data

Railyug

Railway journey assistant · IMPACTX 2026

Risk modellingSimulationAccessibility

A concept and work plan for IMPACTX 2026 Challenge 01 (Reimagining Railway Travel) that treats a rail trip as a living plan and re-plans when things go wrong.

Case study

Problem

Separate tickets for separate legs carry no guarantee that both confirm, so a traveller can be stranded when one leg confirms and the other doesn’t.

Solution

Itineraries are ranked by risk-adjusted arrival with uncertainty ranges. A waitlist estimator reports a confidence range with its sample size, and a dependency model compares keeping, cancelling or re-routing the first leg. Hard rules keep an older passenger and their caregiver together, and a Tatkal hold labels a seat “Processing” for 3 minutes so two people can’t hold it at once.

Highlights

  • Per-passenger connection feasibility and per-leg refund ledger
  • Keyboard and screen-reader support planned from the first screen
  • All data labelled simulated; bookings hand off to official railway channels
